## Overview

You will take the recurring work from your own week and sort it by the distinction from this lesson: ChatGPT is strong at **shaping material** and weak at **sourcing facts**. By the end you will have a shortlist of tasks that are genuinely worth trying, and a clear reason why the others are not.

No tool required yet. We open accounts in the next lesson, and this shortlist is what you will point them at.

## Prerequisites

- None

## Steps

### 1. Write down six recurring tasks

Six things you do at least monthly that involve reading or writing. Be specific. "Communication" is not a task, "writing the weekly status update for my team lead" is.

### 2. Sort each one into a column

| Column | The task is mostly about | Likely outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Shaping | Rewriting, shortening, structuring, translating material you already have | Works well today |
| Sourcing | Supplying facts, figures or current information the model would have to know | Risky without help |
| Judging | Deciding, weighing consequences, taking responsibility | Stays with you |

Many tasks split across columns. Note where the seam runs, because that seam is exactly where you hand over and take back.

### 3. Pick your two best candidates

Choose the two that landed most clearly in "shaping". For each, write one sentence describing what a good result would look like.

That sentence is the beginning of your first prompt, and you will use it again in Lesson 3.

### 4. Find the one that would go wrong

Pick one task that looks tempting but sits in "sourcing" or "judging". Write down what specifically would go wrong if you handed it over unchecked.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Six specific recurring tasks written down
- [ ] Each one sorted, with split tasks marked at the seam
- [ ] Two candidates chosen, each with a sentence describing a good result
- [ ] One tempting-but-wrong task identified, with the concrete failure named

## Troubleshooting

**"Everything I do lands in judging."**
Look one level down. A decision is rarely one step. Preparing the options, summarising the arguments and drafting the announcement are all separate, and usually only the middle one truly requires you.

**"My tasks all need internal information."**
Note them anyway and mark what is missing. In Lessons 2 and 5 we cover attaching files and building projects, which is exactly how that gap gets closed.

**"I cannot think of six."**
Go through last week's sent mail. The pattern shows up faster there than in memory.

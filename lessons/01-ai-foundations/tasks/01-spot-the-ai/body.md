## Overview

You will go through your own working week and identify where machine learning is already making decisions for you. By the end you will be able to tell the difference between software that follows written rules and software that learned from data, and you will be able to name which kind of learning is behind it.

No technical knowledge and no tools are required. Pen and paper is enough.

## Prerequisites

- None

## Steps

### 1. List your tools

Write down five tools or systems you touch in a typical work week. Email, the CRM, your banking app, the intranet search, the photo app on your phone — anything counts.

### 2. Apply the rule test

For each tool, ask yourself one question:

> Could a developer have written this behaviour down as a list of rules beforehand?

If the answer is yes, it is classic software. If the result depends on patterns nobody could have written out in advance, you are probably looking at machine learning.

### 3. Pick three and classify them

Choose the three most interesting candidates and decide which type of learning fits best:

| Type | Recognise it by |
|---|---|
| Supervised | Someone had to label examples with the correct answer first |
| Unsupervised | It groups things together without anyone defining the groups |
| Reinforcement | It improves through feedback on its own attempts |

### 4. Look for the weak spot

Pick one of your three and answer in one or two sentences:

- Which data did this system most likely learn from?
- What would happen if that data were incomplete or one-sided?

### 5. Compare

Share your most surprising find with the group. The interesting cases are usually the ones where people disagree about whether it counts as AI at all.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Five tools written down
- [ ] Each one sorted into "written rules" or "learned from data"
- [ ] Three candidates assigned to a learning type
- [ ] One realistic weak spot described in your own words
- [ ] One example ready to share with the group

## Troubleshooting

**"I can't find any AI in my tools."**
Look at the small features rather than whole products: the spam folder, autocorrect, "customers also bought", face grouping in your photo app, the fraud warning from your bank.

**"I can't decide which learning type it is."**
That is a good sign, not a mistake. Most real products combine several types. Note both candidates and bring the case to the group.

**"Is autocomplete really AI?"**
It depends on the implementation, and that is exactly the point of the exercise. A fixed dictionary is a rule. A suggestion that adapts to how you write is learned. The label matters less than being able to justify your reasoning.

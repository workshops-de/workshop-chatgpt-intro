<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 1: Where to look first</summary>

Start with the things that feel convenient rather than the things that feel technical. Machine learning usually hides behind small helpful features:

- The spam folder in your mailbox
- Suggested replies and autocorrect
- "Customers who bought this also bought"
- Your photo app grouping pictures by face
- Navigation apps predicting arrival time
- Your bank flagging an unusual payment

</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 2: A sharper version of the rule test</summary>

Instead of asking "is this AI?", ask: **what would the rule look like if I had to write it myself?**

Try it for "recognise a cat in a photo". You will get as far as "pointy ears, whiskers" and then run out of rules that survive a cat seen from behind. That inability to write the rule down is the signal.

</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 3: Telling the three learning types apart</summary>

Ask what the system needed before it could work:

- It needed **examples with correct answers** attached → supervised
- It needed **a lot of data but nobody said what was right** → unsupervised
- It needed **many attempts and feedback on each one** → reinforcement

Most everyday products you will find are supervised. That is normal, not a sign you did the exercise wrong.

</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 4: Finding the weak spot</summary>

Ask who or what is missing from the training data. A speech recognition system trained mostly on American English struggles with a Swabian accent. A demand forecast trained on 2019 data did not see the pandemic coming.

The pattern to remember: **a model fails quietly on anything it has not seen before.**

</details>

## Purpose

The longest exercise of the day and the one participants will remember. Everything before it was preparation for this. Success is not a perfect result but an honest one: each person leaves with a working use case and an accurate sense of where it stops.

## Timing

| Phase | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Step 1, choosing and checking | 4 |
| Step 2, running it for real | 12 |
| Step 3, honest assessment | 4 |
| Steps 4 and 5, building and cold testing | 12 |
| Step 6 and sharing | 3 |

If time is short, cut step 5 rather than step 3. The honest assessment is what makes people credible when they report back at work.

## Common Issues

**A demo example instead of real work.** People reach for something safe and generic, and the whole exercise loses its value. Push early for real material, within whatever the data rules allow.

**Perfectionism in step 2.** Some will still be polishing after fifteen minutes. Announce the time and say clearly that usable beats perfect.

**Skipping the honest assessment.** Step 3 gets rushed because the result looks good. Insist on the written time comparison. This is where the inflated expectations of the morning get corrected.

**The cold test is skipped as unnecessary.** It is the most instructive step and the easiest to drop. If you are running behind, at least do it as a live demonstration with one volunteer's project.

**Someone builds a use case that clearly breaches their company's data rules.** Address it immediately and use it as a group discussion. It is far better that this happens in a workshop than next Tuesday.

## Discussion Points

- Who genuinely saved time, and who did not?
- What surprised you about the cold test?
- Which task turned out to be a bad fit, and what did that teach you?
- What would your organisation need to change for this to work properly?

## Bridge to the Next Lesson

Collect the organisational blockers that came up — plans, policies, missing access. They lead directly into Lesson 8 and the personal plan that closes the day.

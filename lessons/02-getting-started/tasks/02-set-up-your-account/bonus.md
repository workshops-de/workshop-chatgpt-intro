Finished early? Pick one or two of these. None of them are required.

## Easy: test a temporary chat

Start a temporary chat and ask it what it knows about you. Compare the answer to the one you got in step 5.

Then close it, reopen your history, and confirm the conversation really is gone. Knowing this works is what makes you willing to use it when it matters.

## Easy: give your custom instructions a real test

Open a fresh chat and ask something deliberately ambiguous, for example *can you help me with the report?*

If your instructions are doing their job, it asks which report instead of inventing one. If it invents one, your instructions need the line about asking before answering.

## Medium: build a second project with a different purpose

Create a project for something completely unlike your first one — a private learning topic, for example.

Then ask each project the same general question and compare. Seeing project instructions change the answer to an identical question is the moment most people understand what projects are for.

## Medium: find your plan's limits

Work out what your plan actually gives you: which reasoning levels appear, whether the tools menu offers deep research, whether you can share a project.

Write the answer down. When a colleague asks whether they need a paid plan, this is the answer they need, and almost nobody has it.

## Hard: write the setup guide for your team

Turn what you just configured into a one-page instruction for a colleague who has never opened the settings.

Name the three settings you consider non-negotiable and say why. If you can write this page, you have understood the lesson better than the exercise required.

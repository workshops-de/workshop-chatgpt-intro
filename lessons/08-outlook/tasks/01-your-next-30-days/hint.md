<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 1: Choosing a task that survives contact with Monday</summary>

The task most likely to stick has three properties: it already happens on a fixed rhythm, you own it alone, and nobody is waiting on the result within the hour.

Weekly reports, meeting notes and recurring internal updates all qualify. Anything urgent or shared does not, because under pressure people fall back to the old way.

</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 2: Questions worth asking your organisation</summary>

- Which ChatGPT plan are we on, and is training disabled?
- May I upload documents containing customer data?
- Is there a written policy, and where does it live?
- Who decides whether we get company accounts?

Ask the specific version. Vague questions about "AI rules" produce vague answers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint 3: A sentence that ages well</summary>

*It saves me real time on first drafts when I give it the material, and I check anything with a number, a name or a date, because it makes those up convincingly.*

Both halves matter. Enthusiasm alone gets you quoted badly, and scepticism alone means nobody asks you again.

</details>

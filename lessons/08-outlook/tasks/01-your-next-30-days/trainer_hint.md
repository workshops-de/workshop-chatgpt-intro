## Purpose

The closing exercise. Its job is transfer, not new content. The deliberate limit to a single task is the whole design: participants who leave with a long list of intentions typically act on none of them.

## Timing

| Phase | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Steps 1 to 4, individually | 9 |
| Step 5, writing the sentence | 3 |
| Closing round, one commitment each aloud | 3 |

The closing round matters more than it looks. Saying a commitment out loud in front of the group measurably raises the chance it happens. Keep it to one sentence per person and do not let it turn into a discussion.

## Common Issues

**People stay vague.** "Use it more" is not a commitment. Push for the task, the day of the week and the calendar entry. Walk the room and check that calendars are actually open.

**The organisational question gets skipped.** Especially in open workshops where people come from different companies and feel it is not their place to ask. Point out that they are now the person in their team who knows the most about this, which makes it exactly their place.

**Someone leaves overpromising.** Watch for participants planning to present this to their whole department next week. Step 5 is the countermeasure. Have them read their sentence aloud and check that it names both sides.

**The mood is flat at the end of a long day.** Normal. Do not fight it with energy, close with substance: remind them what they can now do that they could not this morning, and that the honest assessment they can give is worth more internally than any demo.

## Discussion Points

- What is your one task, and when does it happen next?
- What will you not use it for, and why?
- What does your organisation need to decide before this scales beyond you?

## Closing the Workshop

Collect the organisational questions on a visible list before people leave. For inhouse workshops this list is the most valuable output of the day and belongs in your feedback to the client.

Point people to the workshops.de Discord for questions that come up in the following weeks, and to the follow-up topics: prompting in depth, building agents, and the API for teams that outgrow the chat window.

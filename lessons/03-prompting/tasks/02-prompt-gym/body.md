## Overview

One good prompt proves nothing. This exercise runs the five building blocks across three different kinds of task in quick succession, because that is where the technique actually settles.

Four minutes per round. Speed is the point — you are training a reflex, not producing finished work.

## Prerequisites

- The rebuilt prompt technique from the previous task
- Three pieces of real material, one per round

If you do not have three of your own, the four ready-made materials in the previous exercise cover every round: the email thread for round 1, the product text for round 2, and the meeting notes or the customer feedback for round 3.

## Steps

### Round 1: Summarise

Take something long you actually have to read: a report, a mail thread, a transcript, a set of meeting notes.

Do not ask for "a summary". Name the reader and the decision it serves.

> Summarise the text below for [reader], who has to decide [what]. Five bullet points, each one naming a fact and its consequence. Leave out anything that does not affect that decision.

Four minutes. Then stop, whatever state it is in.

### Round 2: Rewrite

Take a text that exists but is aimed at the wrong audience.

Change exactly one dimension at a time: audience, tone, length or language. Changing all four at once is why rewrites usually disappoint.

> Rewrite the text below for [new audience]. Keep the content and the structure. Change only the level of detail and the assumed prior knowledge.

Four minutes.

### Round 3: Structure

Take something genuinely messy: scattered notes, a half-formed plan, a list of complaints.

This is the round where you have material but no shape.

> Below are my unsorted notes on [topic]. Group them into themes, name each theme, and mark anything that looks like it is missing.

Four minutes.

### Compare

Answer in one line each:

- Which round produced the most usable result?
- Which round needed the most follow-up messages?
- Which of the five blocks did you find yourself reaching for every time?

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Three rounds completed with real material, one of each type
- [ ] Every prompt named both a reader and a format
- [ ] The comparison answered in three lines
- [ ] You can say which building block you rely on most

## Troubleshooting

**"Four minutes is not enough."**
It is not meant to be. The time limit stops you polishing and forces you to notice which prompt got closest fastest.

**"Round 3 gave me something obvious."**
Push it: ask what is missing, ask it to group the same notes a second time along a different dimension. Structuring is where a second attempt pays off most.

**"I do not have three different pieces of material."**
Public sources work: a long article for round 1, a page from your company website for round 2, your own Lesson 1 notes for round 3.

Private material works even better for drilling, because you can judge the result in seconds. A long article you have been meaning to read for round 1, a message you need to write anyway for round 2, and your unsorted notes for a trip, a move or a party for round 3.

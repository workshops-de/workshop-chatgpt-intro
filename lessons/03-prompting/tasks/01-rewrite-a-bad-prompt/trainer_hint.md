## Purpose

The core exercise of the afternoon. The deliberate detour through a bad prompt is what makes it work: participants experience the difference themselves instead of being told about it. The step nearly everyone underestimates is supplying real material, and that is where you should spend your attention while walking the room.

## Common Issues

**People skip the bad version.** Everyone wants to write the good prompt immediately. Insist on step 1. Without the weak answer sitting above it, the improvement is an assertion rather than an experience.

**The rebuilt prompt describes the material instead of containing it.** The single most common mistake. "Summarise our project" instead of pasting the project report. Watch for it specifically and point it out individually.

**The room falls silent and everyone just reads output.** Break it with a request for one person to read their before and after aloud. The contrast is far more convincing from a colleague than from you.

**Someone insists prompting is a fad that better models will remove.** Partly true and worth conceding. Then reframe: the five blocks are not tricks, they are the information the model cannot obtain by any other means. No model generation removes that.

## Discussion Points

- Which of the five blocks made the biggest difference for you?
- Where did the interview approach beat your own prompt?
- What would change if your whole team used the same prompt for this task?

## Overview

You will deliberately write a bad prompt, see what it produces, and then repair it in three passes. Doing it in this order matters: the improvement is only convincing when you have the weak version sitting next to it.

Use one of the candidate tasks from your Lesson 1 shortlist.

## Prerequisites

- A configured account from Lesson 2
- One real task of your own, with material you are allowed to paste

## Steps

### 1. Write the lazy version

One sentence, the way you would type it in a hurry. Something like *write a summary of this project* or *make this email better*.

Send it. Keep the answer.

### 2. Judge what came back

Before improving anything, name the problem precisely:

- Is it too long, too short, or the wrong structure?
- Is the tone wrong for your audience?
- Did it invent anything?
- Is it just generic, and if so, what does it not know?

That last question is the one that tells you what to add.

### 3. Rebuild it with the five blocks

Write a new prompt in the same chat, covering role, task, material, format and constraints. The most important change is almost always the material: paste the actual text or attach the file instead of describing it.

Send it and compare against the first answer.

### 4. Refine in the conversation

Do not start over. Correct what is left with short follow-ups:

- "Half as long."
- "Rewrite the opening, it sounds like marketing."
- "Give me three versions of the final paragraph."

### 5. Try the interview move

Open a fresh chat and, for the same task, write only:

> Ask me questions until you have everything you need to do this well. Then start.

Answer its questions. Compare the result to your carefully built prompt from step 3.

### 6. Save the winner

Whichever approach won, save the working prompt where you will find it again: in your project instructions, in a note, or as the basis for a custom GPT in Lesson 5.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] A lazy prompt sent and its weaknesses named specifically
- [ ] A rebuilt prompt containing real material, not a description of it
- [ ] At least two refinement follow-ups made in the conversation
- [ ] The interview approach tried and compared
- [ ] The working version saved somewhere you will find it

## Troubleshooting

**"The lazy version was already fine."**
Then your task is easy, which is good news. Raise the bar: demand a specific structure, a specific audience, a specific length, and see where it starts falling short.

**"It keeps inventing details about my project."**
It is filling gaps you left. Every invented detail marks a fact you did not supply. Add it and the invention disappears.

**"The interview version asked too many questions."**
Cap it: *Ask me at most five questions, the most important ones first.*

**"I have no material I am allowed to paste."**
Two ways out, and the second is often the better one.

Use a public text: a page from your company website, a job advert, a press release.

Or use something private and low-stakes: a message to your landlord, a complaint about a delayed delivery, an invitation to a family celebration, the itinerary for your next trip. Nothing confidential is involved, and you can judge the result instantly because you know exactly how it should sound. That immediate judgement is what makes the exercise work, and it is often missing with work material you are still unsure about.

Finished early? Pick one or two of these. None of them are required.

## Easy: make it produce something deliberately bad

Ask for the worst possible version of your text: maximum buzzwords, maximum length, no substance.

It is funnier than it sounds and genuinely useful. Seeing the failure mode exaggerated makes it much easier to spot in a mild form later.

## Medium: give it an example instead of a description

Instead of describing the style you want, paste a short piece of text your company already published and ask it to match that.

Compare against your described-style version. For most people the example wins clearly, which is worth knowing before writing another paragraph of instructions.

## Medium: have it grade its own output

Ask it to list three specific weaknesses in the answer it just gave you, then to fix the worst one.

Watch whether the criticism is substantive or decorative. Both outcomes teach you something about how far to trust self-assessment.

## Hard: build the prompt you will reuse

Take your working prompt and turn it into a template with clearly marked gaps, so a colleague could use it on their own material.

The difficulty is deciding what belongs in the template and what has to be filled in each time. That distinction is the core skill behind the custom GPTs in Lesson 5.

## Hard: break your own prompt

Feed it material that does not fit: the wrong document, an empty text, something in another language.

A robust prompt says it cannot do the task. A fragile one invents something anyway. Add the constraint that fixes it and try again.

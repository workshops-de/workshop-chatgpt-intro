Finished early? Pick one or two of these. None of them are required.

## Easy: hand it to your neighbour

Give a colleague your project or GPT and a piece of their material, and say nothing else.

Whatever they have to ask you is missing from your instructions. This is the fastest quality check that exists for a reusable assistant.

## Medium: feed it a difficult case

Run your use case on the awkward example rather than the typical one: the incomplete document, the unusually long input, the case with contradictory information.

Note what it does when the input is bad. Silently inventing a gap-filler is the behaviour that eventually causes real damage.

## Medium: build the second use case

Take the runner-up from your Lesson 1 shortlist and build it too, now that you know the moves.

Time yourself. The second one usually takes a third of the time, which is the number worth reporting when someone asks whether this is worth the effort.

## Hard: add a self-check to the instructions

Extend your instructions so the assistant checks its own output before handing it over, for example by listing every date, figure and name it used so you can verify them at a glance.

This does not make it more accurate. It makes your review faster, which matters more in daily use.

## Hard: write the case for your team

Draft the half page you would send to your manager: which task, how much time it saves, what the risks are, and which decision you need from them.

Nothing in this workshop travels further inside an organisation than a well-argued half page from someone who has actually tried it.
